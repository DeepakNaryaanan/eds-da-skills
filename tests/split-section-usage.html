How DUOPA Works

DUOPA (carbidopa and levodopa enteral suspension) is an intestinal gel that delivers medication continuously for up to 16 hours. The sections below explain the science, the procedure, and what patients can expect.

The Device

A Portable Pump Designed for Daily Life

The DUOPA system uses a small, wearable pump that delivers the gel directly into the small intestine through a tube. Most patients wear it comfortably under clothing during waking hours.

Learn About the Device

Patient Outcomes

More "On" Time. Less Disruption.

In clinical studies, 74% of patients achieved significantly greater "On" time compared to oral levodopa therapy, enabling more daily activity without troublesome dyskinesia.

Review the Clinical Data

The Procedure

How the Tube Is Placed

A gastroenterologist performs a PEG-J (percutaneous endoscopic gastrostomy with jejunal extension) procedure to place the tube directly into the small intestine — typically under sedation as a same-day procedure.

For Healthcare Providers

Identifying the Right Candidate

Consider DUOPA for patients with advanced Parkinson's disease who experience motor fluctuations not adequately controlled on optimised oral levodopa regimens.

Access HCP Resources

Important Safety Information

DUOPA is indicated for the treatment of motor fluctuations in patients with advanced Parkinson's disease. It is not appropriate for all patients. Please consult the full Prescribing Information before initiating therapy.
